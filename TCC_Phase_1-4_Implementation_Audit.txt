TCC — TSQUARE CLOTHING CAFE
COMPREHENSIVE PHASE 1–4 IMPLEMENTATION AUDIT

Audit date: 23 September 2026
Scope: Architecture, Supabase, Authentication, Security, Payments, Wardrobe and Regression Review
Audit type: Inspection only

Overall conclusion: TCC is not yet safe enough for Phase 5.


A. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
====================

TCC contains a substantial Phase 1–4 frontend implementation:

- A complete public-facing site, catalogue, style details, search, bespoke introduction and account UI.
- An eleven-step “Make This Mine” configurator with central client state.
- Supabase browser/server clients, authentication context and account middleware.
- A broad relational schema with RLS enabled in the checked-in SQL.
- Customer-facing requests, orders, measurements, appointments, payments, wardrobe, notifications and concierge interfaces.
- Passing TypeScript and production builds.
- Representative desktop, tablet and mobile routes without observed runtime exceptions or horizontal overflow.

Important strengths:

- auth.users.id is the intended identity anchor.
- Orders, payments and wardrobe records have no customer-write RLS policy.
- Saved styles and wardrobe items have useful uniqueness constraints.
- Requests, orders and wardrobe records attempt to retain historical snapshots.
- No service-role secret or payment secret is exposed in client code.
- Cross-customer reads are logically blocked by the repository RLS definitions, assuming those policies are actually deployed.

Principal risks:

1. Payment success is trusted in the browser. Clicking “Confirm Payment” creates a local successful payment and “verified” receipt without a provider, callback, webhook or server verification.
2. Customers can modify trusted bespoke-request fields, including quote, approval status and administrative notes, through a broad RLS update policy.
3. The request-to-order flow is client-controlled. It hardcodes a quote, changes the request to converted, attempts a prohibited browser order insert, ignores the failure, and displays a local order anyway.
4. Measurement verification and history are customer-writable. A customer can claim TSquare verification, edit earlier versions, and create inconsistent current/version state.
5. Appointment policies allow customers to update confirmation, operational status and confirmed scheduling fields.
6. Live empty accounts can display sample payments, wardrobe garments and staff concierge replies.
7. Most Supabase mutations ignore returned error values, so the application reports success when persistence has failed.
8. No trusted server-side business workflow exists for pricing, orders, payments, production, wardrobe generation, notifications or appointment approval.

There is only a SQL snapshot, not a migration history or a connected Supabase environment.

NOT VERIFIED: Actual production schema deployment, RLS activation, grants, storage configuration and live customer isolation.

These issues must be addressed before introducing Phase 5 administrative authority.

E. DATABASE SCHEMA
==================

All findings describe the checked-in SQL. Deployment is NOT VERIFIED.

profiles
- Purpose: Customer profile linked to Supabase Auth.
- Primary key: id.
- Foreign key: id to auth.users.id, cascading on Auth-user deletion.
- Important columns: first_name, last_name, email, phone, preferred_contact.
- Ownership: auth.uid() = id.
- RLS: Own select, insert and update.
- Risk: Email duplicates Auth email; customers can update all profile columns.

measurement_profiles
- Purpose: Versioned current and historical measurements.
- Primary key: UUID id.
- Foreign key: customer_id to profiles.
- Important columns: version, is_current, unit, fit_preference, verification_status, measurements JSONB, notes.
- Ownership: auth.uid() = customer_id.
- RLS: Own select, insert and update.
- Risks: No unique customer/version constraint; no one-current constraint; verification is customer-writable; history is mutable.

bespoke_requests
- Purpose: Customer configuration and request lifecycle.
- Primary key: UUID id.
- Foreign key: nullable customer_id to profiles with ON DELETE SET NULL.
- Important columns: request reference, style/configuration fields, measurement snapshot, confidence, appointment request, reference images, status, quoted price, clarification/admin notes.
- Ownership: auth.uid() = customer_id.
- RLS: Own select, insert and update.
- Risk: Update covers all columns, including quote, approval status and administrative notes.

orders
- Purpose: Authoritative customer order and historical garment configuration.
- Primary key: UUID id.
- Foreign keys: customer_id to profiles; bespoke_request_id to requests.
- Important columns: order reference, style/configuration snapshots, measurement snapshot, status, total amount.
- Ownership: auth.uid() = customer_id.
- RLS: Own select only.
- Strength: No customer mutation.
- Risk: No trusted creator or status-transition workflow exists.

appointments
- Purpose: Requested/confirmed fitting or consultation appointment.
- Primary key: UUID id.
- Foreign keys: customer, request and order.
- Important columns: requested date/time, confirmed date/time, location, status.
- Ownership: auth.uid() = customer_id.
- RLS: Own select, insert and update.
- Risk: Customer can update confirmation, location and operational status.

saved_styles
- Purpose: Persistent saved style IDs.
- Primary key: UUID id.
- Foreign key: customer_id to profiles.
- Ownership: auth.uid() = customer_id.
- RLS: Own select, insert and delete.
- Strength: Unique customer/style constraint.

notifications
- Purpose: Customer events and read state.
- Primary key: UUID id.
- Foreign key: customer_id to profiles.
- Ownership: auth.uid() = customer_id.
- RLS: Own select and update.
- Risk: Update is not restricted to read fields.

payments
- Purpose: Order payment history.
- Primary key: UUID id.
- Foreign keys: order_id and customer_id.
- Important columns: amount, type, provider, status, provider_reference, internal_reference.
- Ownership: auth.uid() = customer_id.
- RLS: Own select only.
- Strength: Internal reference is unique.
- Risks: Provider reference is not unique; no customer/order ownership consistency constraint.

wardrobe_items
- Purpose: Completed garment history.
- Primary key: UUID id.
- Foreign keys: customer_id and order_id.
- Ownership: auth.uid() = customer_id.
- RLS: Own select only.
- Strength: order_id is unique.
- Risks: No completed-order constraint; no same-customer relationship constraint.

concierge_requests
- Purpose: Customer service thread header.
- Primary key: UUID id.
- Foreign key: customer_id.
- Ownership: auth.uid() = customer_id.
- RLS: Own select and insert.
- Risks: Client-supplied status; related entities are unvalidated text IDs.

concierge_messages
- Purpose: Child messages in a concierge request.
- Primary key: UUID id.
- Foreign keys: request_id and optional sender_id.
- Ownership: Parent request must belong to customer.
- RLS: Own-thread select and customer-message insert.
- Risks: sender_id does not have to equal auth.uid(); the “open request” policy does not check status.

appointment_change_requests
- Purpose: Reschedule/cancellation requests without directly rewriting the original appointment.
- Primary key: UUID id.
- Foreign keys: appointment_id and customer_id.
- Ownership: auth.uid() = customer_id.
- RLS: Own select and insert.
- Risks: Parent appointment ownership is not checked; client can supply an approved/declined status.

Missing application tables/concepts:
- No separate persisted configuration table.
- No reference-upload table or storage metadata.
- No trusted lifecycle/audit-event table.

Constraint and deletion observations:

- Good constraints: Primary keys, most ownership foreign keys, status checks, unique request/order references, unique saved style, unique wardrobe order and unique internal payment reference.
- Missing constraints: One current measurement per customer, unique measurement version, provider transaction idempotency, customer/order ownership consistency, completed-order requirement for wardrobe, related-entity ownership and measurement JSON/range validation.
- Several dates are stored as text.
- Deleting a profile is normally blocked by retained orders/payments using restrictive foreign keys; several other customer tables cascade.
- Deleting a request nulls request links on orders/appointments, preserving their snapshots.
- Payments and wardrobe records restrict order deletion.
- Deleting an appointment cascades its change requests, removing change history.
- Customers have no delete policies for requests, orders, measurements, appointments, payments, wardrobe or concierge data.

G. RLS AND AUTHORIZATION FINDINGS
================================

Assuming the checked-in policies are deployed:

Profiles
- Customer A cannot read/update Customer B.
- Mechanism: auth.uid() = id.

Measurements
- Customer A cannot read/update Customer B.
- Mechanism: auth.uid() = customer_id.
- Customer can still modify trusted fields on their own rows.

Bespoke requests
- Customer A cannot read/update Customer B.
- Mechanism: owner predicate.
- Customer can still modify their own quote, approval status and admin fields.

Orders
- Customer A cannot read Customer B.
- Customer has no insert/update/delete policy.

Appointments
- Customer A cannot directly read/update Customer B’s row.
- Customer can attach known foreign related IDs and update trusted fields on their own appointment.

Saved styles
- Customer A cannot access Customer B’s rows.
- Ownership applies to select, insert and delete.

Notifications
- Customer A cannot access Customer B’s rows.
- Customer can rewrite all fields in their own notifications.

Reference uploads
- NOT VERIFIED. No storage implementation exists.

Payments
- Customer A cannot read Customer B.
- Customer has select only.

Wardrobe
- Customer A cannot read Customer B.
- Customer has select only.

Concierge
- Request ownership and parent-request checks prevent other-thread reads.
- Live behavior remains NOT VERIFIED.

Appointment changes
- The change row is customer-scoped.
- The target appointment’s ownership is not validated during insert.

PostgreSQL uses the USING predicate as the update check when a separate WITH CHECK is omitted. Changing customer_id to another owner is therefore not the central flaw. The central flaw is that policies grant updates to entire rows rather than limiting customers to safe columns/actions.

Customer-writable trusted fields include:

- measurement_profiles: version, is_current, verification_status, measurements and notes.
- bespoke_requests: status, quoted_price, clarification_notes and admin_notes.
- appointments: confirmed date/time, location, related IDs and operational status.
- notifications: event type/content/related IDs, not only read state.
- concierge_requests: status and related IDs.
- appointment_change_requests: target appointment and initial status.

Customers cannot, through the checked-in RLS:

- Insert/update/delete orders.
- Insert/update/delete payments.
- Insert/update/delete wardrobe items.
- Insert system notifications.
- Insert a concierge message with sender_type = concierge.

Real two-user testing against the deployed database was not possible. Deployed grants and policies are NOT VERIFIED.


H. MEASUREMENT ARCHITECTURE
===========================

The intended architecture is versioned measurement profiles plus copied JSON snapshots.

Positive elements:

- measurement_profiles stores version, current state, unit, fit and verification.
- Requests, orders and wardrobe items copy measurement values into JSON snapshots.
- Later changes to the current profile do not automatically alter an order snapshot.

Problems:

- Saving first updates all current records to false, then inserts a new record. The operations are not transactional.
- Returned Supabase errors are not inspected.
- No database constraint ensures exactly one current profile.
- No unique customer/version constraint exists.
- Customers can rewrite prior measurement versions.
- Customers can set verification_status to tsquare_verified.
- Request snapshots remain customer-updateable because the whole request row is updateable.
- Configurator measurementUnit and measurementMethod are not included in the request database snapshot.
- Historical numbers can consequently become ambiguous.

Conclusion:
- Historical snapshot intent is present.
- Reliable immutability, verification and version consistency are not present.
- This is a high-priority repair before Phase 5.


I. BESPOKE REQUEST ARCHITECTURE
===============================

The configurator provides:

Style -> Fabric -> Colour -> Preferences -> Fit -> Measurements -> Occasion -> Required Date -> Appointment -> Customer Information -> Review

Observed behavior:

- Configuration is centralized in tcc_bespoke_config_v1.
- Each update persists to localStorage.
- Back navigation retains unrelated selections.
- A stored configuration is resumed only when its style slug matches.
- Start From An Idea works as a category-first journey.
- Step validation covers required selections, email, phone, dates and manual measurement ranges.
- Review displays selections and permits step editing.
- There is no final trusted/server revalidation.

Reference images:

- Browser object URLs only.
- Maximum six files and 10 MB each are enforced only in the client.
- URLs are not durable after browser restart.
- URLs are not revoked.
- File bytes are never uploaded.
- reference_images is not populated with real stored files.

Submission flow:

1. A local submission is created and configuration is cleared.
2. addBespokeRequest(payload) is called without awaiting it.
3. Browser navigates to confirmation.
4. Authenticated direct browser insert is attempted.
5. Returned Supabase error is not checked.
6. Local state is updated regardless of database success.

Guest behavior is explicitly local/development-only. It is acceptable as a demo but is not operational persistence.


J. REQUEST TO ORDER LIFECYCLE
=============================

The database distinguishes requests and orders, so the conceptual BESPOKE REQUEST != ORDER rule is present. The actual conversion flow is unsafe.

Current conversion behavior:

1. Browser finds a request marked pricing_ready.
2. Browser hardcodes N320,000 as an “authoritative” quote.
3. Browser creates an order reference using time/random values.
4. Browser updates the request to converted_to_order.
5. Browser attempts to insert an order.
6. Order RLS should reject that insert.
7. Error is ignored.
8. Local confirmed order and notification are displayed anyway.

Consequences:

- Customer cannot create a real database order under the checked-in order RLS.
- Customer can create an order that looks real in the browser.
- Customer can directly change their request to pricing_ready, confirmed or converted_to_order.
- Customer can set their own quote.
- Request can become converted in the database while no order exists.
- Inserted order payload omits the request relationship, weakening traceability even if privileged insertion were used.

This violates the trusted review -> pricing -> confirmation -> order boundary.


K. ORDER ARCHITECTURE
=====================

Positive foundation:

- UUID primary key and unique order reference.
- Customer and optional bespoke-request relationships.
- Copied style, fabric, colour, preferences and measurement snapshot.
- Production-status enum.
- Total amount.
- Customer select-only RLS.
- Customers cannot update database order total or production status.

Missing trusted behavior:

- No server action, API route, staff workflow or database function creates an order.
- No trusted process links an approved request to an order.
- No server process changes production status.
- No event/audit table records status changes.
- Only conversion implementation is the unsafe local/client flow.

Order detail isolation is logically sound when live data is used: the client loads owner-scoped rows under RLS and then finds the requested ID. A different customer’s ID should resolve to not found if deployed RLS matches the repository.


L. PAYMENT ARCHITECTURE AND SECURITY
====================================

Integration status: UI PLUS ARCHITECTURE ONLY. NO REAL PAYMENT PROVIDER.

Provider: sandbox only.
Initialization: No provider SDK or hosted checkout.
Server boundary: None.
Callback: None.
Webhook: None.
Verification: Sandbox verify() always returns successful.
Secret handling: No payment secrets exist.
Payment creation: Direct browser insert followed by unconditional local success.
Idempotency: No external transaction uniqueness or retry handling.
Receipt: Rendered from account-store data, including local records.

The payment modal states that payments are securely verified and linked to an authoritative ledger, but then calls recordPayment() with provider = sandbox.

Financial calculations:

- Include only successful payments.
- Support zero, partial, full and multiple payments.
- Exclude pending/failed payments from amount paid.
- Clamp outstanding balance at zero.
- Clamp payment percentage at 100 percent.
- Handle zero total safely.

Limitations:

- Overpayment is hidden by clamping.
- Refund behavior is absent.
- No currency column or explicit minor-unit convention.
- JavaScript numbers are rounded to whole naira while database values permit two decimals.

Database trust boundary:

- Customer has select only on payments.
- Amount, status, provider reference and owner cannot be customer-updated.
- Internal reference is unique.

However:

- Browser attempts a prohibited payment insert.
- Returned error is ignored.
- Local store is updated as successful.
- Receipt and balance trust the local record.
- provider_reference is not unique.
- No webhook/callback idempotency key exists.
- Schema does not enforce payment customer matches order customer.

Conclusion:
- Database RLS should prevent a durable fake payment.
- The customer interface still permits fake successful payment state and an authoritative-looking receipt.
- Severity: CRITICAL.


M. WARDROBE ARCHITECTURE
========================

Positive schema foundation:

- Each wardrobe item belongs to a customer and order.
- order_id is unique, preventing duplicate database items for one order.
- Fabric, colour, preferences and measurements are copied as snapshots.
- Customers have select-only RLS.

Current creation behavior:

- syncCompletedOrderToWardrobe() runs in the browser.
- It does not independently require a completed order.
- It attempts a browser insert that RLS should reject.
- Returned error is ignored.
- It always creates a local wardrobe item and notification.
- No server/database workflow generates the authoritative record.
- Database does not constrain the wardrobe customer to match the order customer or require a completed status.

Order Similar:

- Builds a route using a no-op string replacement.
- Normally supplies a style ID instead of the valid style slug.
- Destination therefore ordinarily resolves to not found.

Use As Inspiration:

- Adds reference_wardrobe to the idea URL.
- Configurator never consumes the query parameter.
- No prefill occurs.

The original order and wardrobe item are not mutated, which is correct.


N. APPOINTMENT ARCHITECTURE
===========================

There are three appointment behaviors:

1. Public /book-a-fitting uses setTimeout and no persistence.
2. Configurator requests an appointment through a direct browser insert, not transactionally linked to the request.
3. Account reschedule/cancellation inserts a separate appointment_change_requests row and leaves the original appointment unchanged.

The separate change-request concept is good because the original remains authoritative until approval.

Problems:

- Customers can update all columns on their own appointment.
- Customers can change confirmation dates, location and trusted status.
- Customers can turn a requested appointment into confirmed, completed or cancelled without staff approval.
- Change-request insert policy does not prove target appointment ownership.
- Customer can submit approved or declined as initial change status.
- Change-request history is not loaded from Supabase.
- Deleting an appointment cascades its change requests.
- Public page says the submission is “verified and processed” although nothing is persisted.


O. SAVED LOOKS
===============

Guest behavior:

- Saved in localStorage.
- Duplicate prevention is present.

At authentication:

- Guest style IDs are upserted into saved_styles.
- Unique constraint prevents duplicate customer/style rows.
- Guest local data is not erased.

Incomplete behavior:

- UI keeps using the local saved-store rather than hydrating from Supabase.
- Removing a save updates localStorage only and does not delete the database row.
- Migration errors are ignored.
- Cross-device persistence is inconsistent.
- Public saved page still says Phase 2 customer accounts are forthcoming.

RLS ownership for saved_styles is appropriately based on customer_id.

Q. CONCIERGE
============

Positive findings:

- Request reads are owner-scoped.
- Message reads require ownership of parent request.
- Customers can insert only messages with sender_type = customer.
- Customers cannot directly insert a staff/concierge message.

Problems:

- New request IDs are generated as conc-<timestamp>, but database ID is UUID.
- Live request insert therefore fails.
- Subsequent message insert also fails.
- Both errors are ignored.
- Local request and notification appear successful.
- Request status is client-supplied.
- Related entities are unvalidated text IDs.
- sender_id is not required to equal authenticated user.
- “Open request” message policy does not check request status.
- Empty live accounts receive a sample concierge request and staff reply.

Customer A should not be able to read Customer B’s thread if repository RLS is deployed. Live verification was not possible.

V. ISSUES BY SEVERITY
=====================

CRITICAL
--------

1. Browser can produce successful payment state and a verified-looking receipt.

Problem:
- No provider or server verifies payment before the UI records success.

Why it matters:
- A customer can fake successful payment state merely by completing a client flow.

Evidence:
- src/lib/account-store.ts, recordPayment(), approximately line 727.
- src/app/account/orders/[id]/page.tsx payment modal, approximately lines 148 and 649.

Affected area:
- Payment modal, account store, sandbox adapter and receipt UI.

Recommended correction:
- Move payment initialization and verification to a trusted server flow.
- Derive successful records only from verified provider events.
- Never update financial UI as successful after a rejected insert.


HIGH
----

1. Customer can update request price, approval status and admin fields.

Why it matters:
- Customers can self-approve, self-price or mark requests converted.

Evidence:
- supabase/schema.sql, bespoke_requests update policy around line 116.

Recommended correction:
- Separate customer-editable fields from trusted transitions and expose trusted transitions only to server/admin procedures.

2. Request-to-order conversion is client-controlled.

Why it matters:
- Request can be marked converted while real order creation fails; a local confirmed order still appears.

Evidence:
- src/lib/account-store.ts, acceptBespokeQuoteAndConvertToOrder(), around line 1107.

Recommended correction:
- Implement one transactional trusted conversion that validates approval/quote and inserts the linked order.

3. Measurement verification and history are customer-writable.

Why it matters:
- Customer can forge TSquare verification, edit history and create inconsistent current state.

Evidence:
- supabase/schema.sql, measurement_profiles policies around lines 42–71.
- src/lib/account-store.ts measurement save around line 595.

Recommended correction:
- Restrict verified/staff fields, make versions immutable, and transactionally enforce one current version.

4. Appointment confirmation and operational status are customer-writable.

Why it matters:
- Customer can bypass staff approval and rewrite trusted scheduling/status.

Evidence:
- supabase/schema.sql, appointment update policy around line 193.

Recommended correction:
- Permit only narrow customer actions and process confirmation/status through trusted workflows.

5. Persistence errors are ignored while UI reports success.

Why it matters:
- Requests, orders, payments, wardrobe and concierge can look complete with no database record.

Evidence:
- src/lib/account-store.ts mutation functions beginning around line 570.

Recommended correction:
- Inspect every Supabase result, stop on error, use transactions where appropriate and show truthful failure state.

6. Sample financial and operational data is injected into real empty accounts.

Why it matters:
- Customers can see fictional payments, garments and staff conversations as though they were real.

Evidence:
- src/lib/account-store.ts fallback mapping around lines 473–539.

Recommended correction:
- Put fixtures behind an explicit development/demo flag. Real empty accounts must display empty states.

7. Wardrobe generation has no trusted completed-order path.

Why it matters:
- Local garments can be forged and database creation does not enforce completion/customer consistency.

Evidence:
- src/lib/account-store.ts, syncCompletedOrderToWardrobe(), around line 797.

Recommended correction:
- Generate wardrobe items from a trusted completed-order transition and retain the unique order constraint.
